## Policy — Commercial Members (Managed Care HMO/POS, PPO, Indemnity)

Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) or deep transcranial magnetic
stimulation (dTMS) of the brain using an **FDA-cleared device and modality** may be
considered **MEDICALLY NECESSARY** as a treatment of major depressive disorder
when **all** of the following conditions (1–3) have been met:

1. **Confirmed diagnosis of severe major depressive disorder** (single or
   recurrent) documented by standardized rating scales that reliably measure
   depressive symptoms; **and**

2. **Any one** of the following (a, b, c, or d):
   a. Failure of **2 trials** of psychopharmacologic agents; or
   b. Inability to tolerate a therapeutic dose of medications as evidenced by
      **2 trials** of psychopharmacologic agents with distinct side effects; or
   c. History of response to rTMS in a previous depressive episode (at least
      3 months since the prior episode); or
   d. Is a candidate for electroconvulsive therapy (ECT); further, ECT would not
      be clinically superior to rTMS (e.g., in cases with psychosis, acute
      suicidal risk, catatonia, or life-threatening inanition rTMS should NOT be
      used); **and**

3. **Failure of a trial of psychotherapy** known to be effective in the treatment
   of major depressive disorder, of an adequate frequency and duration, without
   significant improvement in depressive symptoms, as documented by standardized
   rating scales that reliably measure depressive symptoms.

## Investigational

The following are considered **INVESTIGATIONAL**:

- rTMS/dTMS for major depressive disorder that does **not** meet criteria 1–3 above.
- Continued treatment with rTMS/dTMS as **maintenance therapy**.
- rTMS/dTMS used **in conjunction with esketamine or IV ketamine** treatment.
- rTMS/dTMS as a treatment of **all other psychiatric/neurologic disorders**,
  including but not limited to bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, obsessive-compulsive
  disorder, or migraine headaches.

## Treatment Course Limits

rTMS/dTMS should be performed using an FDA-cleared device in appropriately
selected patients by adequately trained physicians. A treatment course should
**not exceed 5 days a week for 6 weeks (total of 30 sessions)**, followed by a
**3-week taper** of 3 treatments in week 1, 2 treatments the next week, and
1 treatment in the last week.

## Contraindications to rTMS

a. Seizure disorder or any history of seizure with increased risk of future seizure; or
b. Presence of acute or chronic psychotic symptoms or disorders (e.g., schizophrenia,
   schizophreniform, or schizoaffective disorder) in the current depressive episode; or
c. Neurologic conditions including epilepsy, cerebrovascular disease, dementia,
   increased intracranial pressure, a history of repetitive or severe head trauma,
   or primary or secondary tumors in the central nervous system; or
d. Presence of an implanted magnetic-sensitive medical device located within
   30 centimeters of the TMS magnetic coil (e.g., cochlear implant, implanted
   cardioverter defibrillator, pacemaker, vagus nerve stimulator, metal aneurysm
   clips/coils, staples, or stents).

## Prior Authorization

**Prior authorization IS REQUIRED** for the services described in this policy
(commercial Managed Care HMO/POS, commercial PPO and Indemnity, Medicare HMO Blue,
and Medicare PPO Blue). Inpatient services require precertification for all products.
Commercial Managed Care providers must submit the Repetitive Transcranial Magnetic
Stimulation (rTMS) Request Form. For commercial members the request must meet medical
policy guidelines.

## Description (summary)

Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) is a noninvasive method of delivering
electrical stimulation to the brain via a coil placed over the scalp. Repetitive
TMS (rTMS) is used for treatment-resistant depression (TRD); high-frequency
stimulation is typically delivered over the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex
(DLPFC). Deep TMS uses an H-coil to stimulate deeper structures; theta-burst
stimulation uses lower intensities and shorter intervals. For TRD, the evidence
(numerous sham-controlled RCTs and meta-analyses) is sufficient to determine that
the technology improves the net health outcome for patients meeting the criteria
above. Evidence for migraine, OCD, and other psychiatric/neurologic disorders is
insufficient.
